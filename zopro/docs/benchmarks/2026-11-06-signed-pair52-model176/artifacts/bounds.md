# Signed-coefficient bounds and scope

Let B=2^52, with normalized input limbs in [0,B). These are mathematical lane bounds; the Rust screen uses i128 intermediates and asserts the proposed signed-i64 range. It does not execute IFMA or prove a compiler's native implementation.

For i<j, ai*bj+aj*bi=di+dj+(ai-aj)(bj-bi). Orient the first difference to x=abs(ai-aj), and negate the second when the first difference was negative. Then 0<=x<B and -B<y<B. IFMA's truncated second operand yields x*(y mod B). Subtracting x from the next coefficient when y<0 restores the exact signed product. Its low digit is in [0,B), and its corrected high digit lies strictly between -B and B. The intermediate high digit before that subtraction is also in [0,B).

The diagonal polynomial is D(X)*(1+X+X^2+X^3+X^4), where D(X)=sum(di*X^i), since each di contributes once for every other limb and once to its own diagonal. For at most two products, each summed low/high diagonal is below 2B. The six split E coefficients have endpoints below 2B and four interior coefficients below 4B. Their total is below 20B. The prefix/tail schedule, including its temporary full six-term sum, stays in [0,20B). At any power, the ten cross pairs have at most two pairs starting at that power and two ending there, per product. Thus all intermediate convolution coefficients, including uncorrected positive high terms, have absolute value below 20B+8B=28B<32B. This covers arbitrary full-width limbs, not only field values.

Each 52-bit cancellation sets q=t0*(-p0^-1) mod B. Since p0 is odd, q=0 exactly when t0 mod B=0. Otherwise t0 mod B+(q*p0 mod B)=B. Consequently (t0+q*p0)/B=ceil(t0/B)+floor(q*p0/B), including negative t0. The arithmetic-shift ceiling is (t0+B-1)>>52. The row contributes at most two additional IFMA pieces below B to any particular later coefficient. Across all five rows these contributions total below 10B. A coefficient receives the previous row's carry only once. Inductively, if pre-carry values are below 44B in magnitude, that carry is below B+44 in magnitude; hence all pre-shift coefficients are below 32B+10B+B+44<44B. Ceiling numerators are below 45B. The looser declared 64B bound covers all these operations and signed arithmetic shifts.

At the last R256 row, use b=2^48 and q=t0*(-p0^-1) mod b. The same cancellation identity holds. Since q<2^48, q<<4 is a legal 52-bit operand, and high52((q<<4)*p0)=floor(q*p0/b). Other modulus products still use radix52 low/high pieces. Dividing their polynomial by b scales every higher radix52 coefficient by 16. Each scaled coefficient is below 1024B; the last-row carry has magnitude below B+1024, so the sum is below 1025B+1024<1026B. For R260 there are five 52-bit rows and no scaling. All declared bounds are strictly below 2048B=2^63. No i64 minimum or absolute-value corner can occur.

For both primes the exact integer checks establish 4p<R256 and 3p<2^256. Ordinary T<=4p^2 gives 0<=REDC(T)<p+4p^2/R<2p. Two products give T<=8p^2 and REDC(T)<p+8p^2/R<3p. The proof also covers R260 because it has larger R. The difference case is the nonnegative product sum ab+(2p-c)d, with all four original inputs in [0,2p]. Its residue equals ab-cd modulo p. A single subtraction of 2p when necessary restores [0,2p), followed by at most one subtraction of p for a canonical reference comparison.

Signed normalization propagates arithmetic floor carries into the next radix52 coefficient. The final polynomial is nonnegative and below 3p<2^256<B^5. Thus the fifth normalized digit is nonnegative and below B, and there is no sixth digit/final carry. Intermediate normalization adds only a carry below 1026 to a previously bounded coefficient, still within the loose 1026B bound: coefficients at the 48-bit step are below 1025B+1024 before normalization, leaving almost B of headroom. R260 has substantially more headroom.

These inequalities apply to the fixed straight-line recipe. The finite input population supplements them; observed maxima are not a general proof. Native code still needs the signed shifts, masks, final reduction and register allocation checked, plus independent field-oracle tests. The model does not qualify a production field API, instruction timing, stack peak, a mobile device, a full proof or a solver.

Source-operation proxy: each ordinary product has five diagonal and ten difference products, each costing two IFMAs: 30. Fused has 60. Each of five cancellation rows costs one quotient IFMA, one low-modulus high IFMA and eight other modulus IFMAs: 50. Total 80/110, versus the accepted 100/150 source recipe. Orienting and correcting each pair requires seven vector/mask operations (two differences, one abs, one sign mask, one masked negation, one second sign mask and one masked subtraction). Four E additions, four prefix additions, two middle operations and three tail subtractions cost thirteen more. Thus 83/153 additional recipe operations precede common reduction/normalization work. This is neither a complete instruction count nor a throughput, latency or full-proof bound.

Register-pressure obligation for a native prototype: ordinary multiplication must keep ten input vectors and ten output coefficients through the cross-product stage. Diagonal temporary storage must die or be reused before that stage; the modulus can load after the input limbs are dead. Fused multiplication can require twenty input vectors in addition to the coefficients, so scheduling and spills may erase all IFMA savings. The scalar model has no meaningful native stack or liveness measurement. No array size in this model is a proof of low native memory use.
