# Signed Euclidean finish reduction

The existing inverse's one-word Euclidean loop returns coefficients f,g with opposite signs or a zero coefficient, each of magnitude less than2^63. The current modular coefficients u,v are canonical integers in[0,p). Therefore L=f*u+g*v satisfies |L|<2^63*p. The existing linear helper represents this exactly as a signed320-bit value; opposite signs keep each signed128 multiply/add limb intermediate within range. Taking its absolute value is exact because |L|<2^318.

Let B=2^192, d=floor(p/B), e=p-d*B, A=|L| and H=floor(A/B). For either supported BN254 prime d>2^63/3, p<2^255 and H<2^128. Let mu=floor((2^128-1)/d); the exact reciprocal2^128/d is in(mu,mu+1], including a possible power-of-two denominator. q=floor(H*mu/2^128) is at most floor(H/d), and at least floor(H/d)-1, because H<2^128.

Write Q=floor(A/p). Since H>=Q*d, floor(H/d)>=Q. Also H/d<=A/(B*d)=A/p+A*e/(p*B*d)<A/p+2^63/d<A/p+3. Consequently floor(H/d)<=Q+3, and q-Q is in[-1,3]. R=A-q*p is in(-3p,2p). Three conditional additions of p suffice to make R nonnegative, after which R<2p and the existing reduce_once returns its canonical residue. Since p<2^255, this nonnegative result fits four words. Restoring the sign by p-R for nonzero R gives L mod p.

The reciprocal high product uses exact four64x64->128 products. Its middle carry is accumulated in128 bits before adding the next diagonals; q fits64 bits. The modulus product q*p and its subtraction use five64-bit words with exact carry/borrow. Negative corrections include the fifth sign word. No floating-point approximation, variable integer division or table lookup is used by the reducer. All input bounds are checked in diagnostic builds; the production candidate dispatches to the old finish for unsupported modulus high-word bounds.

In the old finish the radix64 cancellation produces L/2^64 mod p, then a Montgomery multiply by the field constant whose numerical value is2^64 restores L mod p. The new path returns that residue directly. The Euclidean loop, signs, initial R2 coefficients,31/62-bit updates and exceptional/zero inverse handling are unchanged. This is a local algebraic derivation, not a claimed published BN254 timing result. The existing inverse follows Pornin's binary-GCD work; the linked practical inversion article was inspected for its algorithm/packing background, not used as a performance prediction.
