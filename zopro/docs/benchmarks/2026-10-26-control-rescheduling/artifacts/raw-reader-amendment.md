Post-capture audit found two display limitations: libtraceevent's scheduler pretty printer omits the preemption bit, and perf script renders two exited outside-task samples with PID/TID -1 even though the raw event retains numeric task IDs. No preemption or complete-coverage claim is made from the first pretty analysis. All old output and its strict usability flags remain intact.

A separate Rust decoder reads the saved perf.data sections directly. It checks the little-endian magic, declared event identifiers/configs, exact sample masks, monotonic clock, record bounds, RAW lengths and the five known tracepoint IDs. It retains the scheduler preemption bit and emits numeric scheduler fields only, without names, addresses or syscall payloads. Sample layout and preemption have synthetic tests; unexpected sample layouts are rejected. Event counts are compared against the independent perf script reader, including its two unparsed lines. LOST and LOST_SAMPLES are read directly. No new proof or scheduler capture is made.

The retained analyzer now reports total continuity errors separately from errors whose uncertain interval intersects a proof clock bracket. The pre-existing strict counts are preserved, and the new proof-window count controls interpretation of in-window metrics. Any in-window inconsistency remains unusable. Caller off-CPU and scheduler-state counts are descriptive additions from the same saved events. Aggregated off-CPU sums overlap across threads and are not removable wall time.
