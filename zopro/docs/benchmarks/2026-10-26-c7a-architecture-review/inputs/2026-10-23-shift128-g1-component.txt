# One high-half shift for G1 quotient MSMs

Rejected at the component stage. One immutable `[2^128]P` per original base reduces the scalar representation from 22 signed 12-bit windows to 11. Both highest halves use indexed reduction to avoid narrow-digit collisions. Neither partition variant improves on the retained implementation. No SDK or production change was made.

Native Ryzen, 32 workers, 31 reversing rounds and three priming calls per mode/size/input type: 992 measured and 96 priming component calls. Distinct generator multiples and full-width scalars; setup and oracle construction are outside timing. Grouped diagnostics include grouping both arrays. The predeclared eligibility target is raw quotient inputs, requiring at least 10% paired improvement against both retained controls at 32,768 points with 21/31 wins, and at most 5% regression elsewhere. Grouped results cannot justify witness-query integration.

232 independent weighted-generator oracle calls passed at two and 32 workers, including 128-bit boundaries, identity/inverse/repeated points, and full-width/grouped scalars. Every measured output was checked after the timer. Source inspection confirms the primary folded quotient has 32,768 points; synthetic component data is still not actual SDK proof data.

The shifted table costs 64 bytes per point, below 4 MiB in the tested range. Setup also creates temporary projective points and normalization scratch; setup peak memory is unmeasured. Existing-budget admission and fallback would be mandatory before integration, which is not justified by these results. No physical-phone, memory-pressure, AWS, or full-proof speed claim is made.

The source review compared Aeglos’s complete-window shifted tables with this single-half approach. Aeglos source hashes are recorded in the protocol. Prior G1 GLV/Horner and G2 shift experiments were not rerun or relabeled. [Raw results](results.json), source/library hashes, executable, and commands are archived. Production pins are unchanged; 9 ms and the full 10× goal remain unmet.
