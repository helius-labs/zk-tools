# Prior evidence reviewed before implementation

The full weighted-SDK study b009b57d11 remains unadopted: a small aggregate OFF/ON improvement does not establish gain against accepted source176. The next scope is the dominant ordinary update streams, not another weighted-tree cutoff or joint frontier.

The G1 prefix-chain study changed chain count/dependency topology; the G2 norm-heap and denominator-tree studies changed product topology. Their failed gates remain failed. The current boundary change removes known-unit products and a dead final update without introducing a tree, chain, reordering or additional scratch. Exact boundary arithmetic, full caller time and generated code still need evidence. It is not a claimed2x approach.

Reviewed source/results: g1-columns176 and column tiles (native segments/circular scheduling regressed); g2-packed-index175 (less CPU, worse proof latency); g1-quarter-current (small positive estimate with failed calibration); g2-norm-heap175 and g2-denominator-tree176 (balanced product dependencies did not establish full gain); G2 preparation and grouping/bypass/radix/Swiss studies; guarded transformed-X feeds and their memory/performance limits. Their failures are preserved, not classified as new work.

New web search re-encountered known papers rather than a new applicable2x method. [SRE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27452) was already implemented/evaluated in earlier bounded/global studies. [Noël/Plantard](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13389-026-00393-z) describes memory-constrained bucket adaptation; the publisher abstract was read, not the inaccessible full text. It does not establish a gain here. eprint2026/2667 and older SIMD/FPGA papers also retain their prior applicability limits. No external implementation or formula is imported into this candidate. A new Rust crate's search snippet suggested batch-affine/window scheduling, but its source was inaccessible and is not used as evidence.

The new implementation is derived directly from accepted source176 and existing field bounds. Complete scalar-reference curve tests precede any timing. No private input/witness/key payload is published.
