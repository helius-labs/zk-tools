# Primary-source audit

The existing project already reviewed [SQIsign AVX-512](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13948v2) and [DigitsOnTurbo](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.21566v1). Their general advice about dependency graphs and conversion costs remains relevant; neither is a new mechanism here. Prefix-only G1, native bucket52, late-coordinate/input-anchor, reference staging, Gauss Fq2 products, deferredDIF32 and parallel-normalization experiments retain their negative/mixed results. This stage does not repeat them.

[Safieh and Freudenberger, Montgomery Reduction for Gaussian Integers](https://www.mdpi.com/2410-387X/5/1/6) develops Montgomery reduction in Gaussian integer residue rings and examines absolute-value and Manhattan-weight corrections. Its implementation discussion concerns protected hardware point multiplication; it supplies no BN254 CPU/IFMA or prover performance prediction. [Safieh etal., efficient Barrett reduction for Gaussian moduli](https://arith2023.arithsymposium.org/papers/An%20Efficient%20Barrett%20Reduction%20Algorithm%20for%20Gaussian%20Integer%20Moduli.pdf) discusses a different reduction route; its abstract was reviewed, not adopted as a backend.

Our concrete proposal is inferred independently from the exact BN scalar modulus identity. Gaussian coordinates could make multiplication by the fourth root of unity inexpensive, enabling a different FFT arithmetic tradeoff. The small imaginary part b also matters to the elementary correction bound. Exact tests and actual native timing, including an eventual conversion/fullFFT qualification, must decide suitability. Saving one butterfly product does not establish a whole-proof gain or a universal memory bound.
